How to use this guide
Work through the steps in order. Each step has a clear goal,
the actions to take, and a deliverable you should have at the
end. Keep a simple folder - digital or physical - where you
store every output.
 What you will need: 
- A legal business entity (LLC, corporation or sole
proprietorship) and your tax ID (EIN or SSN) 
- A dedicated business bank account and routing/account
numbers for EFT 
- A clear description of what you sell and where you can
perform work 
- A PDF editor or printer for forms, plus a consistent place to
store files (Google Drive, Dropbox, etc.)

Step 1: Define your contracting lane
Summarize it all in one page: service + NAICS +
geography + job size + target buyers. Narrow
scope wins faster - you can broaden later.
Choose 1 primary service line - roofing, IT,
help desk, facility maintenance and select 2-4
matching NAICS codes.
Decide geographic reach - local, multi-state,
or nationwide and define your typical job size
range.
List 3 target buyer types to start - Army
installations, VA medical centers, GSA buyers.
Add more once you have past performance.

Step 4: Create your Capability Statement (your one-page resume)
Write 3-5 competencies in plain language -
what you do and who you serve.
List commercial, state/local, or subcontract
work with scope, location, and results. Private-
sector projects count.
Include NAICS codes, UEI, CAGE, business
size/status, and contact details. Export as a
one-page PDF.Add 3 reasons why you - certifications, speed,
specialized equipment, or unique experience.

Step 5: Build an opportunity pipeline (where you will look every week)
Create saved searches on SAM.gov using your
NAICS codes, keywords, and set-aside filters
that match your eligibility.
Log every opportunity by stage: New,
Reviewing, No-Bid, Bidding, Submitted,
Awarded. Aim for 10 active deals minimum.Identify 20 target agencies, bases or
contracting offices. Record key contacts and
note recurring service contracts.
Start with RFQs, simplified acquisitions, and
recurring services. Lower complexity = faster
first wins.

Step 6: Make a bid decision and build a compliant proposal
Read the solicitation top to bottom. Map every
required document and section into a checklist -
compliance wins contracts.Can you meet scope, schedule, location, and key
requirements? If not, pass - chasing the wrong
contract costs time and credibility.
Tailor your technical and management approach to
the buyer's exact problem. Price labor, materials,
travel, and overhead per the requested format.
Submit early via the required channel (email, portal,
or PIEE) and confirm receipt. A late or incomplete
submission is an automatic disqualification.431
